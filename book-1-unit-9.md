# Review

This unit summarizes the main points covered so far. Go over them until you know them to the point where you could explain them to someone else.

## Pronouns

    yo, tú, él / ella / usted, nosotros / nosotras, (vosotros / vosotras), ellos / ellas / ustedes

*Usted* and *ustedes* are formal. They mean *you,* but they are conjugated in third person. We usually omit the subject pronoun, since the verb ending already tells us who the subject is: *estoy triste* instead of *yo estoy triste.*

## Ser and estar

*Ser* is for identity: traits, origin and professions. *Estar* is for states and location.

    Laura es simpática. John es de España. María es secretaria.

    Pedro está cansado. Madrid está en España.

To make a sentence negative, we place *no* right before the verb: *Yo no soy alto. Ellos no están en España.*

## Nouns, articles and adjectives

Nouns have gender and number. We learn them with their article: *el piano, la guitarra.* For the plural, we add *-s,* or *-es* after a consonant.

    el / la / los / las (the)

    un / una / unos / unas (a / some)

In Spanish, generic nouns at the beginning of a sentence need an article: *Los gatos son graciosos.*

Adjectives match the noun in gender and number, and they usually go after it: *la guitarra roja, los pianos rojos.* Some adjectives are neutral for gender: *azul, grande, feliz, triste.*

Possessive determiners go before the noun: *mi, tu, su, nuestro / nuestra, vuestro / vuestra, su.* They take *-s* when more than one thing is possessed: *mis carros, nuestras casas.* There is no *'s* in Spanish: we say *la casa de Juan.*

## Questions

    qué, dónde, cuándo, cómo, quién, cuánto, por qué, cuál

Before *ser,* we use *cuál* (plural *cuáles*) to ask for a piece of information: *¿Cuál es tu color favorito?* We use *qué* to ask for a definition: *¿Qué es un piano?* *Cuánto* matches the noun in gender and number: *¿Cuántas sillas hay en tu sala?* Questions open with an inverted ¿ (and exclamations with ¡), and question words carry an accent (´) on their strong syllable. With *ir,* we often use *adónde*: *¿Adónde vas los lunes?*

## Verbs in the present tense

Irregular verbs to know by heart: *ser, estar, tener, ir* and *hacer.*

For regular verbs, we drop the ending of the infinitive and add the ending for each person:

    -ar: -o, -as, -a, -amos, (-áis), -an

    -er: -o, -es, -e, -emos, (-éis), -en

    -ir: -o, -es, -e, -imos, (-ís), -en

We use the present tense for habits and facts: *Los martes trabajo en mi casa. Mi primo es de Boston.*

*Hay* means both *there is* and *there are*: *Hay un piano. Hay unos pianos.*

## Objects and the personal a

The subject does the action and the object receives it. When the object is a person or a pet, we place *a* right after the verb: *Amo a María. María llama a Jorge.*

## Reflexive verbs

The infinitive ends in *-se.* We conjugate the verb as usual and add the reflexive pronoun before it:

    me, te, se, nos, (os), se

    Me levanto a las siete de la mañana cada día. Laura se maquilla y se peina. Normalmente nos acostamos a las diez de la noche.

For the negative, *no* goes before the pronoun: *Yo no me ducho por la mañana.* *Acostarse* and *dormirse* change O to UE (*me acuesto, me duermo*), except with *nosotros* and *vosotros.* *Vestirse* (*me visto*) and *ponerse* (*me pongo*) are irregular.

## Gustar

The thing we like is the subject, and we are the object: *me gusta* for one thing, *me gustan* for more than one.

    (a mí) me, (a ti) te, (a él / a ella / a usted) le, (a nosotros / a nosotras) nos, (a vosotros / a vosotras) (os), (a ellos / a ellas / a ustedes) les

    Me gustan las manzanas. Me gusta cantar. A mi hermano no le gusta la pizza.

We use *a* before a name or a noun, the infinitive for activities, and the definite article for generic nouns.

## Prepositions, adverbs and conjunctions

    de (from / of), en (in / on / at), a (to), con (with), sin (without)

*A* + *el* = *al*: *Voy al parque.* *De* goes with *ser* for origin, and *en* goes with *estar* for location.

*Para* is for purpose or destination, and with an infinitive it means *in order to*: *La falda es para María. Voy a la escuela para aprender.* *Por* is for duration or exchange: *Estoy en Nueva York por siete días. Compro un vestido por veinte euros.* After a preposition, *yo* becomes *mí* and *tú* becomes *ti*: *para mí, para ti.*

*Mucho* and *poco* are adverbs after a verb (*Eva canta mucho*) and determiners before a noun, matching it (*muchas ventanas*). *Bueno* and *malo* go with nouns, *bien* and *mal* go with verbs. *Muy* (very) is an adverb: *Yo estoy muy cansado.* The conjunctions *y* (and), *o* (or) and *pero* (but) connect words or sentences: *Juan está enojado o cansado.*

## Days, time and numbers

Days of the week are not capitalized. *On Mondays* is *los lunes*; only *sábado* and *domingo* take *-s* in the plural.

    ¿Qué hora es? Es la una. Son las nueve y diecisiete. Son las dos y media. Son las siete y cuarto.

    ¿A qué hora desayunas? Desayuno a las siete de la mañana.

We don't use *am* or *pm.* With a specific time we say *de la mañana, de la tarde, de la noche.* Without one, we say *por la mañana, por la tarde, por la noche.*

From 0 to 30, each number is a single word: *dieciséis, veintiuno, treinta.* From 31 on, we combine them: *treinta y uno, cuarenta y tres,* and so on up to *cien.* For age, we use *tener*: *Yo tengo treinta años.*

## Vocabulary to review

Family members, colors, common adjectives, the house, body parts, clothes, places, time expressions, and the nouns and regular verb lists.
